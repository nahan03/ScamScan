# ScamScan evaluation report

Generated by `python train.py` on the public *Real / Fake Job Posting Prediction* dataset (Kaggle, based on EMSCAD).
All numbers below come from that run. Synthetic data is never used for metrics (plan section 4.3).

## Setup

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Ads loaded | 17,880 (866 fraudulent, 4.8%) |
| Split | 80% train / 20% held-out test, stratified, `random_state=42` |
| Held-out test set | 3,576 ads, 173 fraudulent |
| Classifier | TF-IDF (1-2 grams, 50,000 features) + logistic regression with balanced class weights, then sigmoid (Platt) calibration, 3-fold |
| Rule weight in the combined score | 0.3 (model 0.7) |
| "Flagged" means | model alone: probability at least 0.5. Rules and combined: score at least 0.25 (medium) or any hard flag |

For rule evaluation the dataset's scrubbed `#URL_...#` and `#EMAIL_...#` markers are replaced with a neutral link and a company-style email. Without this the rules see "no contact" in almost every ad. The classifier sees the original text. Because the real email domains are unknown, **the free-email rule cannot be tested on this dataset**.

## Results (held-out test split)

| Method | ROC AUC | Avg precision | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rules alone | 0.401 | 0.044 | 0.036 | 0.416 | 0.066 |
| Model alone | 0.988 | 0.935 | 0.971 | 0.786 | 0.869 |
| Combined (30% rules, 70% model) | 0.973 | 0.919 | 0.869 | 0.884 | 0.877 |

**Success criterion (plan section 1.6):** combined recall of at least 0.70 at precision of at least 0.50. **Met** (recall 0.884, precision 0.869).

**Not yet measured:** the second criterion, at least 80% agreement with your own labels on a hand-labelled UAE-style set. That set does not exist yet. See `data/README.md` and run `python eval_uae.py data/uae_labeled.csv` once you have collected 30 or more ads.

## What the evaluation taught us

1. **The rules score below chance on this dataset (ROC AUC 0.40).** The dataset is mostly non-UAE postings with contact details removed. It contains almost none of the patterns the rules target (7 fee requests and 5 bank-detail requests in 17,880 ads). The rules that do fire often either hit nearly every ad (`no_verifiable_contact`, 99%) or point the wrong way: `urgency` fires on 9.5% of all ads but only 4.5% of the fraudulent ones. This shows the dataset cannot judge the rules. It does not show the rules are bad on UAE-style ads, and we deliberately did not tune them to fit this data.
2. **The first combined score was badly calibrated.** With the original 60/40 weights and an uncalibrated classifier, the combined score had precision 0.187 at recall 0.988. Balanced class weights inflate probabilities far above the real 4.8% fraud rate, so a clean detailed ad scored "medium". Two changes fixed it. Lowering the rule weight to 0.3 raised precision to about 0.42 at recall 0.95 (measured in an experiment while building M4, not part of `train.py`). Calibrating the classifier then raised it to 0.869 at recall 0.884.
3. **The rule weight was cut from 0.6 to 0.3** because of point 1. It is one constant (`RULE_WEIGHT` in `scamscan/constants.py`). On this dataset the rules slightly lower the combined AUC (0.973 versus 0.988 for the model alone). Revisit the weight when the UAE-style set exists. Hard flags (fee request, bank, card or OTP request) still force "high" regardless of weight.

## Known failure cases

The first two rows were observed by running the app on the made-up sample ads in `tests/samples.py`. The others follow from how the tool is built and have not been measured:

| Case | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An ad with no company name and no contact details but no other warning sign | Scored **low** (10/100). Rules alone would say medium (28). | The classifier gives it a very low probability and now carries 70% of the score. |
| A UAE-style scam (fee request, passport and bank details by WhatsApp) | Classifier probability is only 0.36. The result is **high** only because the hard-flag rule forces it. | The classifier was trained on a different style of ad and does not recognise WhatsApp-style wording. |
| A fee request phrased in a way the patterns do not cover | Not flagged as a hard flag. | The rules use a fixed list of phrasings plus a simple negation check ("no registration fee"). |
| An ad written in Arabic or mixing languages | Not analysed meaningfully. | English only (out of scope for the MVP). |
| A genuine employer who uses a free email and writes a short ad | Can be flagged medium (the made-up vague free-email sample scores medium, 27/100). | By design the tool shows a risk level and reasons, not a verdict. |
